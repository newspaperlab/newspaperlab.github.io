NVIDIA unveils scientific computing rack built around 192 Vera CPUs

Vendor systems are expected in early 2027. The HPC SDK and a public beta Fortran compiler arrive first.

Edited by Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-17 07:00 KST
Source published 2026-11-16

A data-center platform is more than one CPU benchmark. NVIDIA announced at SC26 on November 16 a liquid-cooled rack for scientific computing that connects 192 Vera CPUs. Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro systems are expected beginning in early 2027.

Eight CPUs fit in each 1U tray. The rack combines about 300TB of memory, 1PB of storage and more than 200TB/s aggregate peak memory bandwidth over Quantum-X800 InfiniBand. Those are rack totals, not guaranteed throughput for one application.

Each Vera CPU has 88 custom Olympus cores, 176 threads and SOCAMM2 LPDDR5X memory at up to 1.2TB/s. SHARP in-network computing aims to reduce coordination work across nodes.

NVIDIA reports 1.9x performance and 2.4x performance per watt versus x86 on an OpenFOAM motorBike Large test, and projects 1.6x performance and 2x efficiency on WRF. These are vendor tests and projections tied to configurations and optimization.

Germany’s DKRZ selected Vera for its HLRE-5 supercomputer, expected in early 2028, alongside GB200 systems and Quantum-2 networking. It is a heterogeneous system rather than a universal CPU replacement.

HPC SDK 26.11 is available now and a next-generation Fortran compiler is in public beta. Price, shipment volume and site power/cooling requirements remain undisclosed. Buyers need code-specific tests and full infrastructure costing.
