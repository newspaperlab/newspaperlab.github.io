PlayStation delays Pulse Elevate speakers with no new launch date

A production issue was found during quality testing. The former November 12 release is no longer valid.

Edited by Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-16 07:00 KST
Source announced 2026-11-06

If a launch-day order does not move, it may not be an account error. Sony Interactive Entertainment updated its PlayStation Blog notice on November 6 to say a production issue was found during quality testing and the Pulse Elevate wireless speakers were delayed. The original November 12 date is struck through, and no replacement date is given.

Pulse Elevate is presented as SIE’s first wireless desktop speaker system. Planned features include planar magnetic drivers, built-in woofers, a right-speaker microphone with AI noise rejection, simultaneous PlayStation Link and Bluetooth connections, rechargeable batteries and charging docks.

Those specifications are separate from retail readiness. Sony confirms a production issue but does not identify the component or process, say how existing units will be handled or promise a simultaneous relaunch in every market. There is no basis to label the undisclosed issue a safety defect.

Previously announced Southeast Asian prices included SGD319, MYR1,099, THB8,499, PHP14,709 and VND6,999,000, with availability varying by market. Preorders began September 2. A retailer’s order record still controls charging, delivery and refund terms.

Preorder customers should check whether their retailer keeps, cancels or refunds the order and whether a new confirmation will be required. The confirmed change is simply that November 12 is no longer the launch date. Monitor the official post and retailer notice rather than relying on an estimated replacement date.

If you planned to share the speakers between PC and PlayStation, recheck final compatibility. PlayStation Link needs supported hardware and an adapter setup, and simultaneous Bluetooth does not promise every possible audio mix. Wait for the final manual before buying extras.

A pre-sale delay can indicate that quality control caught a problem, but it does not reveal the repair timeline. The official scope is limited to a production issue found in testing and no new date; component cause and inventory treatment remain unknown.
