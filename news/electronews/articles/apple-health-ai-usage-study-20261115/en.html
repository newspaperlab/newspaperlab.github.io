Apple opens U.S. health AI usage study for up to 200,000 participants

Eligible participants are 21 or older with an Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone. The app is research, not diagnosis or treatment.

Edited by Newspaper Lab · Published 2026-11-17 07:00 KST
Source published 2026-11-15

People increasingly ask AI health questions, but trust and action are separate issues. Apple launched the Apple Health AI Usage Study in the United States on November 15, a virtual study for up to 200,000 people examining trust, comprehension, response quality and subsequent health actions.

Participants use a separate app built with OpenEvidence technology to ask everyday questions and see linked peer-reviewed clinical citations. The research also examines whether conversational tools help people prepare for visits with clinicians.

Eligibility is U.S.-based, age 21 or older, with an Apple Intelligence-capable iPhone 15 Pro or later running iOS 26 or newer. The App Store’s 18+ rating is not the same as the study’s 21+ enrollment rule.

Participants may choose whether to share HealthKit data such as resting heart rate, activity, sleep or lab results, and survey responses. Apple says study data will not train AI models. Read enrollment screens to distinguish required and optional data.

The app includes guardrails intended to direct certain concerning questions to a provider or other resources. It is not intended to diagnose, treat or replace medical advice. Urgent symptoms require ordinary emergency or clinical care.

Up to 200,000 is a recruitment ceiling, not current enrollment. Apple has not announced result timing, expansion outside the U.S. or integration into the standard Health app. Review consent, retention, withdrawal and contact details before joining.
